**Cyprus ends the summer holding 111.7 mln. m³ — 38.4% of capacity, and two and a half times what it had a year ago. But the recovery came less from rain than from the gentlest summer drawdown of any year on record that had water worth losing. Thirty-nine years of data say the comeback is real. They also say this is a thoroughly average August.**

---

The last week of summer is the moment Cyprus traditionally looks at its reservoirs and winces. This year it can afford to look.

According to the Water Development Department's bulletin of 21 August, the island's 18 main dams hold **111.7 mln. m³ — 38.4% of capacity**. On the same week last year they held 43.6 mln. m³, 15.0%. The reserve has grown by **68.1 mln. m³ in twelve months, a factor of 2.56** — the seventh-largest year-on-year swing in end-of-August storage since records began in 1988.

There is a blunter way to say it. **There is more water in Cyprus's dams today, at the driest point of the year, than there was at the wettest moment of the whole of 2025.** Last year's high-water mark was 77.4 mln. m³, reached on 15 January. Today's figure is 44% higher — and today is the day the reserve is at its thinnest.

The calendar year has been violent in the good direction: **27.4 mln. m³ on 5 January** — the lowest reading since February 2009 — **123.4 mln. m³ by 4 June**, and 111.7 mln. m³ now.

### The part nobody has noticed

The year-on-year comparison is the story every outlet will run. The more interesting number is what happened after the rain stopped.

Between 15 June and 21 August the dams gave up **11.3 mln. m³ — 9.2% of what they were holding.** That is the fourth-gentlest summer drawdown in thirty-nine years, behind only 2019, 2020 and 2022, and it happened at a rate of 0.169 mln. m³ a day.

Put that rate against every other year in the record and something unusual appears. **Of the twenty-seven summers since 1988 that began with 100 mln. m³ or more in storage, 2026 drained the slowest.** The only two years that lost water more slowly — 2008 and 1998 — did so because there was almost nothing left to lose. In August 2008 the island's dams were down to 18.8 mln. m³.

Across the summer the reserve has fallen by an average of **169,000 m³ a day — about 1,955 litres every second.** Over the same stretch last year it was 2,630 litres a second. **The island is emptying 26% slower than it was twelve months ago.**

{{chart:summer-drawdown}}

Two things explain it.

**Summer was not dry.** Inflow across June, July and August reached **4.73 mln. m³ — the second-wettest summer in eleven years**, behind only the exceptional 2018/19 season. The August–September figure alone, at 0.55 mln. m³, is already the highest in that eleven-year table with September still to come. There was even a measurable event in the third week of August: **0.33 mln. m³ arrived between the 13th and the 21st**, nearly all of it into [Kouris](/dam/kouris/). Water flowing into a Cyprus dam in mid-August is not a normal occurrence.

| Season | Jun + Jul + Aug inflow (mln. m³) |
|--------|--------------------------------:|
| 2018/19 | 8.27 |
| **2025/26** | **4.73** |
| 2019/20 | 3.77 |
| 2017/18 | 2.15 |
| 2021/22 | 1.39 |
| 2024/25 | 0.08 |

**And the dams peaked absurdly late.** The reserve did not reach its seasonal high until **4 June**. Of the thirty-seven years in which the dams refilled at all, **only one other — 2009 — was still filling in June.** Thirty-five had peaked by the end of May, and the commonest month to peak is April. The dams were still taking on water when most years are already two months into decline, and a shorter drawdown season means a shallower one.

*(A note on precision: readings before 2014 were taken twice a month, on the 1st and the 15th, so peaks can only honestly be compared by month, not by date.)*

### And now the cold water

None of which makes 2026 a good year. It makes it an ordinary one.

**111.7 mln. m³ is 11.1 mln. m³ below the thirty-nine-year average for late August**, which is 122.8 mln. m³, or 42.2%. Ranked against every year since 1988, **2026 comes twenty-first of thirty-nine** — the middle of the pack, and slightly on the dry side of it. It is the healthiest end of summer since 2023, but 2023 itself finished the season on 165.8 mln. m³, half as much again as today.

The comparison survives the obvious objection. Strip out the reservoirs built since 1988 — Kannaviou and the three recharge dams — and the like-for-like figure is 103.7 mln. m³ against a like-for-like average of 117.8 mln. m³. Same verdict.

### The water is in the wrong dams

The recovery has also been wildly uneven, and it favoured the reservoirs that matter least.

The small mountain dams are essentially full. [Kalopanagiotis](/dam/kalopanagiotis/) stands at **98.6%**, [Klirou-Malounta](/dam/klirou-malounta/) at 91.9%, [Solea](/dam/solea/) at 90.1%, [Tamassos](/dam/tamassos/) at 87.6%, [Xyliatos](/dam/xyliatos/) at 85.0%, [Vyzakia](/dam/vyzakia/) at 82.2% — a reservoir that was at 0.7% this time last year. Between them, those six hold about 11 mln. m³.

The [Southern Conveyor](/region/southern-conveyor/) system, which accounts for 65% of national capacity, sits at **35.9%**.

**Kouris and [Asprokremmos](/dam/asprokremmos/) alone hold 55% of every drop in the island's reservoirs.** Kouris is at 39.6%, Asprokremmos at 38.5%. Those two numbers, not the 98.6% at Kalopanagiotis, decide what next summer looks like.

And one corner of the island got almost nothing.

| Reservoir | Now | Last year | Change |
|-----------|----:|----------:|-------:|
| [Achna](/dam/achna/) | **1.4%** | 4.6% | **−3.2 pp** |

| [Dipotamos](/dam/dipotamos/) | 30.4% | 28.1% | +2.3 pp |
| [Kalavasos](/dam/kalavasos/) | 23.2% | 16.0% | +7.2 pp |

Achna, at **1.4%**, is the only reservoir in Cyprus holding less water than it did a year ago. It is filled by transfer rather than by its own catchment, so its level reflects allocation decisions rather than rainfall — but it is still, at the end of the wettest winter in years, effectively empty.

### August is not the bottom

The last thing worth saying is that the season everyone treats as the low point isn't one.

Cyprus reservoirs do not bottom out in August. They bottom out in late November or December, after two more months of demand and before the first serious rain. Over the past eleven years the island has lost a further **13.7 to 32.2 mln. m³ between the start of September and the annual minimum**, with a median of 21.9 mln. m³.

Apply that to today's figure and **Cyprus is on course to bottom out somewhere near 90 mln. m³ — around 31% — in late November.**

Which, in the end, is the number that matters. Last winter the island bottomed at 26.9 mln. m³. **The coming water year is set to open with more than three times the buffer the last one did.**

That is a real cushion, and it was not there twelve months ago. But it is a cushion, not a solution: the structural picture is unchanged, roughly 60% of dam reserves still go to agriculture, and one generous winter does not undo four dry ones. What happens after November is a question about rain, and nobody has ever been able to answer it in August.
